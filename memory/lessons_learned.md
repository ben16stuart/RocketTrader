## Standing Rules — Trading

1. **No catalyst = no trade.** Volume alone is never enough.
2. **🥇 ENTER ON THE SECOND DAY.** Same-day entries are **0-for-3** (CSTL, FF, VELO); second-day continuations are **2-for-3** (MRLN, OMER; **ETON −1.4%**). Both same-day losses failed identically — Rocket bought the retrace of a failed opening spike and called it a base. ⚠️ **Corrected 2026-08-21 from "2-for-2" — ETON was also a second-day entry.** But grade the *cause*: ETON's entry was **+4.7% four sessions later**; it lost only because of the exit (lesson 32). **The second-day preference survives; the perfect record does not.**
    - **2a. Opening-range gate (2026-08-14):** if the **9:30–9:45 range exceeds 10% of price**, there is no tradeable 9:45 base — **defer to day 2.** VELO ran $17.61→$14.35 (22.7%) in ten minutes; the "base" at $15.27 was the middle of a range. Deferring would have bought 8/13's $14.95 open, now $16.16 (**+8.1%**).
    - **2b. The 9:45–9:50 bar must close in the upper half of its own range.** OMER's did ($17.02/$17.38/$16.92/**$17.33**, 1.6x 5-min volume). That is what a base looks like.
    - **2c.** Gap 20–35% = gap-and-go, not a chase. **Gap >35% = second-day only.**
3. **Second-day rule**: gapped >25% yesterday + **closed above the day's midpoint** → today's open IS the entry, within 10% of prior close. Do not wait for a pullback that won't come.
4. **Close-position-in-range is the tiebreaker** (2026-08-07). Below the midpoint on heavy volume = distribution = dead, catalyst regardless. ASPN (28% of range) and EVH (9%) both had real news and both died; QNST/RCEL/CRSR closed 86–92% and held. **Validating the catalyst is no longer the hard part — surviving day one is.**
    - **4a. Applies to same-day entries too, and it beat Rocket's own stop** (2026-08-12, re-verified 8/14). VELO closed at ~25% of its post-entry range; Rocket closed it same-day at $15.09 for −$5.40. The 7% trail sat at **$14.72** and **8/13's low was $14.65** — holding would have stopped out for **−$16.50**. The discretionary close won by $11.10 *even though VELO later recovered to $16.16.* Run the range check at market_close on every satellite.
    - **4b. Corollary — a 7% trail is inside the noise band of a 20%-range stock.** On such a name the stop is a coin flip, not risk management. That is an argument against entering, not for a wider stop.
5. **🚫 A beat WITHOUT a raise is a DISQUALIFIER, not a demotion** (2026-08-14). 3-for-3 as a fader: SVCO, CVRX, FF. FF closed day one at 30% of range and went $6.37→$5.85→$5.68→$5.59.
6. **🚫 P2 is not a substitute for a failed P1** (2026-08-14). If the P1 fails its gate at the open, the default is **no satellite**, not the next name down. FF was ranked P2 in premarket *for the exact reason it lost* (no raise, no coverage) and was entered only because APEI failed and a slot was open. **A slot being open is not a thesis.**
7. **Rank on the BALANCE SHEET, not the growth rate.** Growth makes a stock gap; funding structure decides whether it holds the gap over a 1–5 day hold.
    - **7a. 🆕 Score the announcement-to-balance-sheet ratio** (2026-08-18). When a contract/partnership catalyst is **orders of magnitude larger than the company's cash**, the catalyst *is* the dilution event — delivering it requires capital the company does not have. **AGPU: $21.9M cash and falling equity against $1.3B of announced contracts (~60x), with an S-3 effective three weeks earlier.** DUOT: $2.7M quarterly revenue against a $500M contract needing a 55 MW build. **Both had strong price action; both were uninvestable.** Divide the announcement by cash before believing it.
    - **7b. 🆕 A circular counterparty voids the contract as evidence.** AGPU pays DUOT to host **while contemplating minority equity in the DUOT SPEs it is paying** — each books the other's side as a headline win. **When a contract's counterparty is also its financier, the revenue is not independent.** Skip both sides, never just the weaker one.
8. **The dilution check runs FIRST and is Rocket's most profitable rule** — one search. Kills: SVCO, STLN, NNBR, RCEL, NRGV — all then traded badly. **A 403 or paywall is not a clean bill of health.**
    - **8b. 🆕 An S-8 is NOT an offering — do not let it trip an exit trigger** (2026-08-18). OMER's 8/13 S-8 was reported as a "$142.67M ESOP-related shelf registration"; it registers **employee-benefit-plan shares** and does **not** draw the ATM. The offering trigger is a **424B5 or an announced deal**, not any filing with "registration" in the headline. **Cuts both ways: read the form type, not the headline.** A false positive sells a good position; a false negative holds a diluting one.
    - **8c. 🆕 An S-3ASR is the fastest takedown vehicle there is — grade it ABOVE an effective shelf** (2026-08-19). An **automatic** shelf registration (WKSI-only) is **effective on filing, with no SEC review** — the issuer can price overnight with zero lead time. **BW was dropped on this alone** (S-3ASR 5/21). Full ladder, safest → worst: **no shelf on file** (ETON) → S-3 pending → S-3 effective → **S-3ASR (immediate)** → **424B5 drawn**.
    - **8d. 🆕 Read the form's SUBJECT, not the form's reputation** (2026-08-19). BW's **25-NSE (delisting notice) filed 8/13** looks like distress; it covers the **6.50% Senior Notes due 2026 that matured**, under Rule 12d2-2(a)(1), and does not touch the common. Pair with 8b's S-8. **Three form-type misreads in one week, in both directions** — a scary form name is not evidence, and a boring one is not absolution. Open the filing.
    - **8a. Grade the dilution *structure*, not its existence** (2026-08-12). Worst → best: **(i) converts struck at a VWAP-DISCOUNTED price** (holders are paid to short strength — automatic kill, e.g. NRGV); **(ii) a drawn shelf with <1yr runway** (RCEL — kill); **(iii) an undrawn ATM with multi-year runway** (VELO, OMER — a risk to size around, not a disqualifier); **(iv) a completed raise months old** (not an overhang at all). **Always date the raise.**
9. **Short float >15% + a catalyst = size up.** Run the check on **every** board candidate, not just the ones that look like squeezes — it is one field in data already being fetched.
10. **Sub-$3 stocks are traps.** Watch names sitting within ~5% of the floor.
11. **The analyst-ladder check is Rocket's best entry filter — now 4-for-4.** Strike both profit targets (+15%, +25%) against a **dated** consensus before the open, every name, every session. A capped ladder is close to sufficient for a skip; an uncapped one is necessary but not sufficient. Kills: QNST (−5.1%), CRSR (−4.5%), HLIT, **CURI (−11.0% on 8/14, closed at 4% of range)**.
    - **11a. An undated analyst headline is not evidence** — resolve it against a dated consensus page. "Barrington raised QNST to $29" was a prior period; the current page showed a $24 reiteration.
    - **11b. 🆕 An UN-RUNNABLE filter is a FAIL, not a pass** (2026-08-14). FF had **no analyst coverage at all**, so the ladder check could not run — and its absence was scored as an absence rather than as a skip. Same logic as rule 8's "a 403 is not a clean bill of health."

27. **🆕 Range collapse on enormous volume = a pinned deal price. Check the bar before researching** (2026-08-19). **WEAV** topped the scanner at **+31.9%, 123.6x RelVol** — and its daily bar was **O $7.29 H $7.32 L $7.28 C $7.28 on 49.4M shares.** A stock cannot absorb 34x normal volume inside a **$0.04 range** unless the price is pinned to a fixed number, and it was: a **$7.40/share all-cash take-private**. **The bar identified the deal faster than the news search did.** Upside is contractually capped, downside is the whole premium if it breaks — no momentum edge, ever. **One glance at high−low on any huge-RelVol gainer, before spending any research on it.**
28. **🆕 A one-session move in the IWM−SPY factor carries no information** (2026-08-19). The factor changed sign **five times in six sessions** (−0.52 → +0.66 → +0.32 → −0.44 → +0.72 → −0.58). On 8/18 this file recorded the Russell-lagging flag as having "stopped flashing"; **it reversed back on the very next session, harder.** Read the factor **weekly in the attribution**, where the ~59% core weight makes it matter — not daily, where it is noise. **This cuts both ways: do not call one red session a bearish small-cap signal either.**

29. **🆕 Screen the EVENT CALENDAR as its own gate — a clean balance sheet does not save a name with a hostile calendar** (2026-08-20). **ARCT** passed everything that normally kills: uncapped dated ladder ($18.29–$23.30 vs $10.28), rule 13 irrelevant ($288M cap), and the **best balance sheet screened to date — $191.5M cash against a $288M market cap, runway guided through YE2028, shelf undrawn** (the inverse of AGPU/DUOT's ratio failure). It was still a skip, because **ARCT-810 Phase 2 data is guided "later in Q3 2026" — undated, and inside any 1–5 day hold.** 🚨 **A 7% trailing stop cannot protect against a data gap; it fills at the open, wherever the open is.** Every prior kill this month came from *funding structure*; this is the first from *timing*. **Check for an undated binary readout before believing a good balance sheet.** Same standing treatment as CAPR: re-screen only once the event is a fact.
30. **🆕 Read the transaction CODE and the SIZE, not the form type — insider threads that look identical resolve in opposite directions** (2026-08-20). OMER and ETON both carried an unresolved "some Form 4s and a 144" thread for four sessions. Opened both the same session; they resolved **opposite**:
    - **OMER = benign.** 8/18 Form 4 was **code A — a GRANT** of 30,000 options to a director. 8/17 Form 4 was **code M then code S — a cashless exercise-and-sell** (5,000 @ $3.93 → 5,000 @ $16.95), **~$85k against a 13.4M-share day = 0.04% of one session's volume.** Compensation mechanics, zero information. **Stop citing it.**
    - **ETON = real.** A **Form 144 for 100,000 founder shares / $6,343,000 by the CEO**, sale date 8/19 — **16% of that day's 610k volume**, and it explains the reversal (H $63.83 → C $61.48 at 38% of range) that `market_close` had searched for and attributed to "an ordinary FOMC-day pullback." **A wrong benign explanation is worse than an open question.**
    - **Extends 8d.** The form *type* is not the signal; **code A (grant) and M+S (cashless exercise) are noise, a sized 144 by the founder is signal.**
    - **30a. A Form 144 is NOT dilution — grade it on a different axis.** It is a *secondary* sale of existing shares: ETON's shares outstanding stayed **28,583,135**, so cap math, rule 13 and the balance sheet are all untouched. It creates **supply pressure/overhang**, which caps advances — an argument to **honor the scale-out rung**, not an exit trigger. Keep it strictly separate from S-3/424B5 solvency risk. ETON's dilution profile is still the cleanest screened to date.
31. **🆕 A whole category can be excluded on MANDATE, and it will keep winning the scanner** (2026-08-20). The entire top tier of the 8/20 tape was **crypto-treasury proxies** (DFDV, HYPD, USDE, SBET, ASST, ABTC, FWDI, BKKT, SECZ). Their catalyst is "a token went up" — not on the catalyst list — and they are **ATM-financed by construction**: issuing stock to buy tokens *is* the business model, so **lesson 8a category (i)/(ii) applies by design, not by accident.** DFDV closed at **93% of range on 3.5x volume**, the best bar on the board. **Second time in three sessions that the prettiest price action was structurally uninvestable** (AI-datacenter theme, 8/18). **When a whole theme tops the scanner, check the mandate before the chart.**
32. **🚨 REWRITTEN 2026-08-21 — the ETON close was WRONG, and the version of this lesson that called it a win was graded on a counterfactual nobody checked.** The 8/20 entry claimed the exit "materially better than where the 7% trailing stop would have triggered." **The bars falsify it in one call**: HWM $63.83 → **stop $59.36**; 8/20 low **$59.59**, 8/21 low **$59.76** — **never touched** — and ETON closed 8/21 at **$63.47**. Holding = **+$19.74**; the close **cost $25.83 = 0.82% of book**, four times the loss it avoided. Three rules come out of it:
    - **32a. A discretionary close may only override a trailing stop when the stop is >2% AWAY.** The 8/20 note itself recorded a "~0.7% cushion" — so the close could save at most 0.7% if the stop fired, against unlimited give-up if it didn't. **That is a bad trade in expectation regardless of outcome, and the asymmetry was computable at the time.** Measure the distance to the live stop before overriding it.
    - **32b. This violated lesson 30a, written the day before** — "a Form 144 is NOT dilution… an argument to honor the scale-out rung, **not an exit trigger**." It was used as exactly that trigger, on a name whose beat-**and**-raise catalyst was intact and whose dilution profile was the cleanest screened to date. **A rule written yesterday is still a rule.**
    - **32c. 🥇 GRADE COUNTERFACTUALS AGAINST THE BARS.** Every "it would have been worse" claim in the trade log must cite **the actual stop level and the actual subsequent low.** This is lesson 33 (an undated headline is not evidence) turned inward: **a counterfactual in Rocket's own log is a claim like any other, and self-grading is where it is least likely to be checked.** Cost of checking: one script call. Cost of not checking: a lesson that would have justified repeating the error.
    - **Standing tally — the end-of-day close rule (4/4a) is 1-for-2**: VELO **+$11.10**, ETON **−$25.83**, net **−$14.73**. Range-position did *not* separate them (both closed ~11–25% of range). What separates them is 32a: VELO's stop was reachable, ETON's was not.

33. **🆕 Rule 11a extends from ANALYST headlines to CATALYST headlines — date the news against the bars before it reaches the board** (2026-08-21). An overnight search returned *"Digital Turbine (**APPS**) surges 22% after hours on a beat-and-raise, adj EPS $0.15 vs $0.05 consensus"* — the **one catalyst type that actually trades** (rule: beat-AND-raise), on a name Rocket already knew. **The article carried no date.** The daily bars killed it in a single call: 8/18 C **12.12** → 8/19 C **11.23 (10% of range)** → 8/20 C **10.71 (6% of range)** on **0.5x** volume — **−11.6% in two sessions, closing at the bottom of its range both days, on below-average volume.** The print was weeks old and fully faded; what looked like a fresh gap was a distribution slide. **Same failure mode as QNST's "Barrington raised to $29," which was a prior period** — only the input is a news story rather than a rating. **A search result describing a move is not evidence until the bars confirm the date.** Cost of the check: one script call.
34. **🆕 Lesson 28's logic applies to RATES: one session of relief is not a resolution either** (2026-08-21). On 8/20 `market_context` recorded the 10-yr as having "resolved the week's key number the right way" — 4.71% → **4.65%** through the FOMC minutes, widening the cushion to the 4.75% trigger from 4bp to **10bp** — and stood the standing rate flag down a notch. **It was back at 4.70% the very next session, cushion re-narrowed to 5bp.** The stand-down was premature by exactly one day. **Lesson 28 said a one-session move in the IWM−SPY factor carries no information; the same restraint applies to any single-session macro print near a trigger.** ⚠️ **Note the asymmetry: this cuts against declaring *relief*, not just against declaring alarm.** Flags near a threshold stand down on a *trend*, never on one print.
35. **🆕 A correct rule can cost money for several sessions running — record the tally, never relax the gate** (2026-08-21). **ARCT** was skipped 8/20 on lesson 29 (undated ARCT-810 Phase II readout inside the hold window) plus rule 1 (no catalyst). 36. **🆕 When a mandate-excluded theme owns the scanner, a no-trade streak is a BOARD-QUALITY problem, not a discipline failure — say so explicitly** (2026-08-21). Crypto-treasury proxies topped the scan a **third straight session** (**USDE +19.3% on 26.1x RelVol**, the biggest print on the board, plus DFDV/ABTC/SBET/ASST/FWDI/SECZ/BKKT/GEMI/HYPD). While tokens rally, **the top tier of every scan is structurally unavailable to Rocket**, so the real board must be built from the names *beneath* it — which is precisely the tier where stale catalysts and extended-hours quotes live. **The two failures compound**: the usable board shrinks at the same moment its average quality drops. **Name the cause in the session note.** Three no-trade sessions with a written reason each is a functioning filter; the thing to watch for is the opposite — reaching down into the weak tier to fill a slot, which is rule 6 (a slot being open is not a thesis).

37. **🆕 Score DAILY RANGE ÷ 7% as a gate at STEP ONE, alongside the universe checks — it is computable from the bars you already pulled, and it killed a name only after five research calls** (2026-08-26). **CAPR** passed everything expensive: universe gates, **rule 13** ($482M, +25% → $602M), **rule 11** (uncapped — $8.29 vs Oppenheimer **$54** / Cantor **$28**), **rule 29** (PDUFA re-dated to **2026-11-22, +88 days, outside the hold window**), and a **top-of-ladder rule-8 profile — no S-3/S-3ASR/424B5 anywhere in 2026, $237.9M cash against a $482M cap.** On paper one of the best-screened names of the month. **It died on rule 4b**: daily ranges of **18.6% / 26.7% / 35% of price** put the mandated **7% trailing stop deep inside the noise band**, so the stop is a coin flip rather than risk management — which 4b says is *an argument against entering, not for a wider stop.*
    - **37a. The check is one subtraction on bars already in hand.** `(high−low)/price` for the last 3–5 sessions, versus the 7% trail. **If the typical range exceeds ~10%, the stop cannot do its job and the name is untradeable by Rocket regardless of how good the catalyst is.** Run it in the same pass as the eligibility gates. Cost of checking first: zero extra calls. Cost this session: **two web searches and an EDGAR dig** spent on a name the bars had already excluded — the same "screen then check" inversion lesson 17a called out.
    - **37b. 🆕 A new kill AXIS, not just a new kill.** Every prior kill this month came from **funding structure** (AGPU, DUOT, RCEL, BW), **calendar** (ARCT, CAPR-as-was), or **cap ceiling** (IE, ETON, APPS). This is the first from **instrument volatility** — the name is fine, the *stop* doesn't fit it. **Keep it on its own axis; a great balance sheet cannot rescue an untradeable range, exactly as a great balance sheet could not rescue ARCT's calendar.**
    - **37c. An FDA review EXTENSION is a delay being repriced as optionality, not a catalyst delivered.** CAPR's run was analysts marking up a *postponement* that followed a **9–3 negative advisory-committee vote**, with a **securities fraud class action filed the same day as the +21.9% move.** Grade the underlying event, not the reaction to it.
38. **🆕 "Could not confirm" is a FAIL — go to the primary source, and prove the source isn't broken before believing a blank** (2026-08-26). The CAPR dilution search returned *no ATM/shelf information found*. Per **rule 11b** that is an un-runnable filter, i.e. a fail — so it went to EDGAR's submissions API directly. ⚠️ **A blank query result is itself a lesson-15 risk**: the first EDGAR query printed *nothing*, which reads identically to "clean." **Re-ran it printing the total filing count (961) and all 56 filings in 2026 to prove the file was populated** — only then was the absence of any 424B5/S-3 real evidence. **Search said unknown; the primary source said clean, and the name kept a top-tier dilution grade it would otherwise have lost.** 🆕 **This cuts the opposite way from rule 8's usual direction** — 8's "a 403 is not a clean bill of health" stops a *false pass*; this stops a **false kill**. Both reduce to: resolve the gap, never score it.
    - **38a. Same method closed the OMER threads the same session.** ATM confirmed **undrawn** (no 424B5 in 2026), and the new **8/20 / 8/21 Form 144s sized at 0.90% and 0.41% of their days' volume** (acquired-date = sale-date, paid cash = **cashless exercise-and-sell**, 0.040% of shares out) — **benign, per lesson 30, against ETON's real one at 16% of a day's volume.** Two open threads closed for the cost of two curl calls.

## Standing Rules — Instruments and tooling

15. **A metric that renders as `nan`, `0.0%`, or `unknown` is a broken instrument, not a cosmetic glitch.** Silent degradation is the failure mode that costs money; loud errors get fixed.
16. **Verify a fix against a live artifact before declaring the problem solved.** The core sleeve was declared to have fixed idle cash on 7/28, before one rebalance had run. Cost: −0.49% in Week 32. ✅ **Closed 8/10–8/14**: three fractional rebalances, all within $0.20 of target, cash drag down to −0.04%.
17. **Pull raw 5-min bars to judge a base; raw daily bars for carry-overs. The scanner is a NAME SOURCE**, and its prices/`Change %` are extended-hours quotes.
    - **17a. 🆕 Cleanest demonstration yet — nine names, nine falsifications, one script call** (2026-08-21). The 04:20 scan showed nine non-crypto names up **+3.4% to +7.7%**. Raw daily bars showed **every one** was an extended-hours quote against a **red or flat 8/20 cash session**: UNCY closed at **15% of range**, **LFMD at 0%** (on the low, 0.3x volume), HDSN at **4%**, OSS at **10%** and −16% in four sessions, ALMU **−20% in three**, **BBW's open WAS its high**. **Not one had a genuine fresh volume-plus-catalyst event.** 🚨 **Run the bars BEFORE building the board, not after** — the loop that screens a name and then checks it has the cost backwards.
18–19. **`alpaca_client.py close SYMBOL` always fully liquidates** — no `--qty`, and it silently ignores one. Use `sell SYMBOL QTY` for partial reductions; verify `qty` in the returned order JSON. **Fractional orders work; trailing stops reject fractional qty by design** — size satellites in whole shares, only the stopless IWM core may be fractional.
20. **Log exits the same day they happen.** CAMP was closed pre-merge with no price, date or reason and is permanently unrecoverable.
21. **🚨 Token budget is a TRADING risk.** Bull and Rocket share one Claude quota across 10 launchd jobs. **<5 searches → inline, never a subagent** (~171k tokens of overhead each).
22. **A plist that exists is not a job that runs — check `launchctl list`, not `ls`.** Midday has not executed since **2026-06-15**.
23. **🔴 Rocket cannot measure Rocket.** `portfolio_snapshot.py` derives Rocket's return from a fixed 30% of the *shared* account — algebraically the whole account's return, Bull's P&L included. Snapshot reads **+0.10% vs SPY since rebase; the truth is −0.78%.** The error grew 67bp → **88bp** in one week. **Trust only the hand-built weekly attribution.**
    - **23a. 🆕 The hand-built BOOK, not the slice, is the valid daily base** (2026-08-14). Every daily note Mon–Fri chained off the contaminated slice ($3,152.62) instead of the W32 review's hand-built book ($3,135.02), and so reported +0.09%→+0.53% all week against a true −0.78%. **Carry the hand-built book forward transaction by transaction.**
24. **`positions` and `portfolio_snapshot.py` round share counts for display** (IWM printed "10" against a real 9.5031). Pull the raw position before computing the rebalance band.
26. **`portfolio_snapshot.py`'s weekly trade counter is broken and undercounts to zero** — `count_weekly_trades()` needs `line.split("—")` to yield ≥3 parts but trade_log headers contain one em-dash. The hardcoded cap (**3**) also doesn't match the real guardrail (**5**/week). **Count trades from `trade_log.md` by hand.**
