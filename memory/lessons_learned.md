# Rocket Lessons Learned

Rules derived from real trades. Read every session.
**Compressed 2026-08-28 — full text and worked examples: `memory/archive/lessons_history.md`.**

## Standing Rules — Trading

1. **No catalyst = no trade.** Volume alone is never enough. (RMNI 8/28: 98% of range on 2.2x, zero catalyst — that is the trap shape, not a signal.)
2. **🥇 ENTER ON THE SECOND DAY.** Same-day 0-for-3 (CSTL, FF, VELO); second-day 2-for-3 (MRLN, OMER; ETON −1.4%, an *exit* failure). **2a.** 9:30–9:45 range >10% of price → no tradeable base, defer to day 2. **2b.** The 9:45–9:50 bar must close in the upper half of its own range. **2c.** Gap 20–35% = gap-and-go; **>35% = second-day only.** **3.** Gapped >25% + **closed above the midpoint** → today's open IS the entry, within 10% of prior close; don't wait for a pullback that won't come.
4. **Close-position-in-range is the tiebreaker.** Below the midpoint on heavy volume = distribution = dead, catalyst regardless. **4a.** Applies to same-day entries too; it beat Rocket's own stop on VELO (+$11.10). **4b.** A 7% trail is inside the noise band of a 20%-range stock — **an argument against entering, not for a wider stop.**
5. **🚫 A beat WITHOUT a raise is a DISQUALIFIER, not a demotion. 5-for-5 as a fader** (SVCO, CVRX, FF, LTRX ×2). 🆕 **5a. SIZE THE RAISE — "raised" is not a binary.** PD's FY revenue guide moved **$492.5M → $494M = +0.3%, on revenue FLAT year-on-year**, and Rocket's own files logged that as *"Rung 1, the only type that has made money."* **ETON's — the trade that earned rung 1 its status — was $120M+ → "exceed $145M."** A sub-1% nudge on flat revenue is nearer a beat-without-a-raise than a real raise. **State the raise as a percentage in the log entry, or the label does the reasoning for you.** 🆕 **5b. Second live application, 2026-09-03 — CHPT.** A genuinely strong beat (revenue $116.1M **+18% YoY**, above the guided $100–110M; adj. loss $1.35 vs $1.60; record 38% GM; EBITDA loss $22.1M → $4.8M) with a forward guide of **$105–115M, midpoint $110M vs consensus $109.3M = +0.6%** — **inside the same sub-1% band as PD's +0.3%.** Worse than PD in two ways: **no FY guidance was issued at all**, and the guided midpoint is **−5.3% BELOW the quarter just reported**, with the whole range topping out under Q2 actual. **Sizing the raise turned an impressive-looking print into an obvious kill in one arithmetic step.** Without 5a, "beat and guided above consensus" is a headline that reads like rung 1.
    - **5c. A guide that is below the quarter it follows is not a raise in any framing** — check the guide against **both** consensus *and* the reported quarter. Consensus alone can be a lowered bar.
    - 🆕 🥇 **5d. CHECK THE GUIDE AGAINST THE COMPANY'S OWN PRIOR GUIDE, AND ASK WHETHER A BIG QUARTERLY BEAT WAS PASSED THROUGH TO THE YEAR** (2026-09-04, SWBI). 5a says size the raise against consensus; that is not enough, because consensus can be stale. **SWBI guided Q1 to +15–20% and delivered +32.3% — a beat against its OWN number of 12–17 points — then moved the full-year guide from "mid-single digits" (≈5%) to "5–7%" (mid 6%): +1 point of growth, ≈+0.95% on the revenue line.** A company that smashes its own quarter and declines to pass any of it through to the year **is telling you the beat was pull-forward, not run-rate** — and SWBI said so explicitly by guiding **Q2 to +10% against a Q1 that just printed +32.3%, a 22-point guided deceleration.** The pass-through ratio is the diagnostic: **beat your own quarter by 15 points, raise the year by 1 → the raise is cosmetic.** 🚨 **Rule 5a is now 3-for-3 as a kill in seven sessions: PD +0.3%, CHPT +0.6%, SWBI +0.95% — all three inside the same sub-1% band.**
    - 🆕 **5f. A REINSTATED GUIDE IS NOT A RAISE, EVEN WHEN THE HEADLINE SAYS "GIVES FY
      OUTLOOK"** (2026-09-04, NX). Quanex withdrew FY26 guidance in Q2 (visibility) and
      "gave" it back today at the **identical $1.84–1.87B / $240–245M range issued in
      March** — a beat-and-headline-outlook that reads like rung 1 but is a **0% change**
      once compared to the pre-withdrawal number, not the post-withdrawal silence. **Always
      diff against the last guide that existed, not the last guide that was stated** — a
      withdrawal is not a reset to zero. ✅ **GRADED CORRECT IN TWO SESSIONS**: NX printed
      the strongest bar on the 9/04 tape (+22.23% on 6.68×, 90% of range) and was logged as
      an uncomfortable carry — it then closed **−3.10% on 9/08.** **A strong close is not a
      credit that offsets a failed guide (42a), and the tape confirmed it.**
      🆕 **5f is broader than withdrawals — a REAFFIRMATION is the same 0% change** (2026-09-09,
      **AVO**). Mission Produce beat the high end of its own Q3 adj-EBITDA guide by $0.4M
      (**+1.25%**), announced a headline "synergy target raised to >$30M" — and then
      **REAFFIRMED** the second-half range at **$84–88M**, with the Q4 guide simply absorbing
      the beat. 🚨 **The tell is that the raised synergy number never reached the guide: if it
      were real and near, the half would have moved.** A forward promise is not a delivered
      result. **Diff the guide, not the adjective in the headline.**
    - 🆕 **5e. READ THE COMPOSITION OF THE BEAT — a one-off larger than net income means the one-off IS the beat.** SWBI's headline was adj EPS **$0.06 vs −$0.05** and GM **28.7% vs 25.9%**. But a **$2.9M non-recurring tariff refund lifted GM ~260bps of the 280bps reported**, and **net income was $2.6M — less than the refund.** Strip it and the quarter is roughly breakeven on roughly flat margins. **A beat assembled out of a non-recurring item does not recur, which is the entire premise of a momentum continuation trade.** Cost of the check: reading one sentence of the release past the headline.
6. **🚫 P2 is not a substitute for a failed P1.** If the P1 fails its gate, the default is **no satellite. A slot being open is not a thesis.**
7. **Rank on the BALANCE SHEET, not the growth rate.** **7a.** Score the announcement-to-cash ratio — when a contract is orders of magnitude larger than cash, **the catalyst IS the dilution event** (AGPU ~60x, DUOT). **7b.** A circular counterparty voids the contract as evidence; skip both sides.
8. **The dilution check runs FIRST and is Rocket's most profitable rule** — one search. **A 403 or paywall is not a clean bill of health.** **8a.** Grade the *structure*, worst→best: VWAP-discounted converts (auto-kill) → drawn shelf, <1yr runway → undrawn ATM, multi-year runway → completed raise months old. **Always date the raise.** **8b.** An **S-8 is NOT an offering** — don't let it trip an exit. **8c.** An **S-3ASR is effective on filing, no SEC review** — grade it above an effective shelf. **8d.** Read the form's SUBJECT, not its reputation (BW's 25-NSE covered matured notes, not the common). **8e.** A press release touting CASH may be advertising the DILUTION that created it — LTRX's "$60M cash, no debt" was **two 424B5 takedowns off a live shelf, three months earlier.** Cost of checking: one curl.
9/10. **Short float >15% + a catalyst = size up** — run it on **every** candidate, it's one field in data already fetched. **Sub-$3 stocks are traps**; watch names within ~5% of the floor.
11. **The analyst-ladder check is Rocket's best entry filter — 5-for-5.** Strike both rungs (+15%, +25%) against a **dated** consensus, every name, every session. 🥇 **OOMA is the cleanest case: killed because the Street's highest target ($24.00) sat below the +15% rung — it then spiked to $26.19 THROUGH that target and closed $23.04, within $0.04 of the $23.00 mean. The ladder named the closing price.** **11a.** An **undated** analyst headline is not evidence — and when dated named targets contradict an undated "consensus," **the dated ones win** (QNST; PD 8/28). **11b.** An **un-runnable** ladder is a FAIL, not a pass (FF had no coverage).
27. **Range collapse on enormous volume = a pinned deal price.** WEAV: +31.9% on 123.6x RelVol inside a **$0.04 range** = a $7.40 all-cash take-private. **One glance at high−low before researching any huge-RelVol gainer.** Same logic kills merger-of-equals names (SSTK/Getty): upside pinned to an exchange ratio.
28. **A ONE-SESSION move in the IWM−SPY factor carries no information** — it changed sign 5 times in 6 sessions. **Read it weekly in the attribution**, where the ~90% core weight makes it matter. **Cuts both ways.** 🆕 **A six-week drift is a different object from a one-session flip** — W35's weekly read (−2.50% since rebase) is the signal this rule was built to permit, and it went to the user rather than into a self-approved change.
29. **Screen the EVENT CALENDAR as its own gate** — a clean balance sheet does not save a hostile calendar. **A 7% trailing stop cannot protect against a data gap; it fills at the open, wherever the open is.** ARCT had the best balance sheet screened to date and was still a skip on an undated Phase 2 readout.
30. **Read the transaction CODE and the SIZE, not the form type.** Code A (grant) and M+S (cashless exercise) are noise; a sized Form 144 by a founder is signal (ETON: 16% of a day's volume). **30a. A Form 144 is NOT dilution** — it's supply pressure, an argument to **honor the scale-out rung, not an exit trigger.**
31. **A whole category can be excluded on MANDATE, and it will keep winning the scanner.** Crypto-treasury proxies are ATM-financed by construction. **When a theme tops the scanner, check the mandate before the chart.**
32. **🚨 A discretionary close that overrides a trailing stop must be graded against the bars.** ETON's close cost **$25.83** against a stop that was never touched. **32a. Only override a stop that is >2% AWAY** — otherwise the asymmetry is bad in expectation regardless of outcome. **32b.** It also violated 30a, written the day before. **32c. 🥇 GRADE COUNTERFACTUALS AGAINST THE BARS** — cite the actual stop level and the actual subsequent low. **Self-grading is where a claim is least likely to be checked.** Standing tally: the end-of-day close rule is **1-for-2**, net −$14.73.
33. **Date the NEWS against the bars before it reaches the board.** An undated "APPS surges 22% on a beat-and-raise" was weeks old and already −11.6% in two sessions at the bottom of its range. **A search result describing a move is not evidence until the bars confirm the date.** **34.** Same for macro: the 10-yr "resolved the right way" and was back at the trigger the next day — **flags near a threshold stand down on a trend, never on one print.**
35. **A correct rule can cost money for several sessions running — record the tally, never relax the gate.** ARCT's skip cost peaked at ≈**+45%** and the gate held. ✅ **VINDICATED 8/28: first red day, −6.1% at 50% of range, volume exhausted 2.1x → 0.6x, readout still undated.** Being right slowly is only possible if you can carry an embarrassing number without flinching.
36. **When a mandate-excluded theme owns the scanner, a no-trade streak is a BOARD-QUALITY problem, not a discipline failure — say so explicitly.** The usable board shrinks exactly as its average quality drops. ⚠️ **But see 41: "the board is empty" is only true of the board you actually looked at.**
37. **Score DAILY RANGE ÷ 7% as a gate at STEP ONE**, from bars already in hand. **37a.** `(high−low)/price` over 3–5 sessions vs the 7% trail; **typical range >~10% = untradeable regardless of catalyst.** Zero extra calls. **37b.** A new kill AXIS — instrument volatility, separate from funding structure and calendar. **37c.** An FDA review **extension** is a delay being repriced as optionality, not a catalyst delivered.
38. **"Could not confirm" is a FAIL — go to the primary source, and prove the source isn't broken before believing a blank.** EDGAR's first query printed nothing, which reads identically to "clean"; re-running it with the filing count (961) proved the file was populated. **Stops false KILLS, where rule 8 stops false passes. Both reduce to: resolve the gap, never score it.**
39. **A factual error in Rocket's OWN memory files propagates unchallenged — verify the named entity, not just the date.** Two sessions called Jackson Hole "Powell's keynote"; the chair is **Kevin Warsh**, which made the event *more* dangerous. **39a.** The same skepticism must run against Rocket's own prior notes: **a dated entry copied forward three times is one unchecked claim read three times.** **39b.** 🚨 Compounds with [[launchd-quota-contention]]: when premarket is starved, the files keep being *read* but stop being *corrected*.
40. **Rule 37a measured on PRE-catalyst bars understates the POST-catalyst range.** LTRX passed the stop-fit gate on 4.8–6.7% trailing ranges, then printed a **21.1% catalyst-day range (3.7x)** and blew through a 7% trail from the open. **40a.** Apply a catalyst-day multiple; trailing ranges are a **floor, never an estimate.** Names with trailing range >~3% are suspect. **40b.** This makes the gate **stricter for quiet names, not looser for loud ones.** **40c.** A name saved by two rules while a third mis-scored it is a **rule failure, not a win.**
41. **🆕 🥇 SOURCE RUNG-1 CATALYSTS FROM THE EARNINGS CALENDAR, NOT FROM A SCREENER** (2026-08-28). **PagerDuty (PD)** reported 8/27 after the close — a **beat AND a raise** (FY rev guide → $494M, FY adj EPS → $1.35), the one catalyst type that has ever made Rocket money. It traded 8/28 **+9.5%, 69% of range, 2.7x volume.** 🚨 **It appears in NONE of Friday's three session notes.** Premarket declared the board "empty by measurement" — correct on its inputs, PD hadn't gapped yet — and market_open's overlap tier returned NABL/OSG/BBW, all three correctly killed on catalyst. **The week's one qualifying name was never on the board at all.**
    - **41a. This is a COVERAGE gap, not a discipline gap — and it is the cheapest gap on the book to close.** Earnings are on a **calendar, published in advance**. Rocket was waiting for beat-and-raise names to bubble up through a volume screen that reports extended-hours quotes as prices (rule 17a, **8 straight falsifications**), printed `0.0x` RelVol for **17 of 20** names, and **returned empty output twice in one week.**
    - **41b. 🚨 A degraded instrument feeding a "no signal" conclusion is indistinguishable from a real absence.** That is lesson 38's blank-result trap operating at the level of the whole session, not one query. **Every premarket now pulls the overnight/before-open earnings reporters and asks "was guidance RAISED?" BEFORE running any screener.**
    - **41c. It qualifies rule 36.** "The board is empty" is only ever true of *the board you looked at*. Naming the cause is necessary but not sufficient — **check that the board was built from a source that cannot silently go blank.**
    - ✅ 🥇 **41d. THE FIX PAID OFF ON ITS SECOND USE** (2026-09-09). The Nasdaq
      earnings-calendar API returned **24 reporters for 9/08 AMC and 45 for 9/09 BMO**, which
      screened to **four in-universe names carrying real dated catalysts (CAL, OCC, INNV, AVO)**
      — on a session where the scanner's entire overlap tier was **one** usable row (OCC, dead
      on a 55% spread) plus a standing mandate kill. **The instrument that was supposed to
      source rung-1 catalysts sourced four, and the screener sourced none.** 🚨 **And note the
      inverted failure mode this creates: 9/08's board was empty by measurement, 9/09's was the
      fullest in a week and produced no trade because every name failed a NAMED gate. Those are
      different outcomes and must be written differently** — "nothing qualified" after screening
      four dated catalysts is a real result; "nothing qualified" from a blank board is 41b.

42. **🆕 WRITE THE KILL/PASS CONDITION IN ADVANCE, THEN HOLD YOURSELF TO IT** (2026-08-31). Friday's open thread pre-committed: *"if the Street marks up above $17.29 the picture genuinely changes."* Monday the Street **did** mark PD up (Truist $13→$15, Canaccord $10→$15) — and landed **at** the high, mean **$12.64, below the current price**. **Because the bar was written before the evidence arrived, an upward revision could be read as the kill it actually was rather than as encouragement.** A gate specified after seeing the data is not a gate. **42a. Independent gates do not net out.** PD passed dilution at the cleanest tier ever screened and was still a skip on the ladder — **a clean balance sheet is not a credit that offsets a capped ladder.** **42b. Ladder now 6-for-6**, and the two cleanest cases (OOMA, PD) share one shape: **the Street's HIGHEST target sitting below the +15% rung.** That specific configuration is the strongest kill signal on the book.
43. **🆕 COUNT THE ROWS AGAINST THE TICKERS REQUESTED.** `eligibility PD NEOV RMNI SAIC FLWS SPIR` printed **five** rows, **silently omitting PD**, and **exited 0**. Re-run alone, PD returned normally. **A missing row reads identically to a clean run** — lesson 38's blank trap, now inside a tool that is trusted precisely because it is mechanical. **The omitted name was the session's primary candidate.** Cost of the check: counting to six. 🚨 **43a. SECOND OCCURRENCE, 2026-09-03**: `eligibility MEI LE MTRX GCO DLTH WLY PHR AGX` requested **8** and returned **7 — MEI omitted, exit 0.** Re-run alone, **MEI passed every gate.** Twice now the dropped row was a name that mattered. **This is a reproducible tool defect, not bad luck — the count is mandatory, every call.** ✅ **43b. Did NOT recur 2026-09-04** (8 requested → 8 returned; 5 → 5). ⚠️ **But I briefly believed it had, because I had piped the call through `tail -40` and the "missing" rows were my own truncation.** **Count against the FULL output — a count run on a truncated view manufactures the exact false positive the rule exists to catch**, and a false alarm here costs re-runs and, worse, erodes trust in a check that has twice been right.

44. **🆕 THE TWO REBALANCE BASES DISAGREE, AND NOBODY NOTICED BECAUSE THEY USED TO AGREE** (2026-09-01). `target_core` can be struck off the **slice** (30% of live shared equity × 90%) or off the **hand-built book** (lesson 23a). On 9/1 they give opposite instructions on the same position: slice says IWM is **+0.30% — in band, no trade**; book says **+3.77% — outside the 3% band, SELL.** **8/31's market_close used the slice and BOUGHT 0.3704 sh.** They diverge because the book is carried forward transaction by transaction while the slice is 30% of an account that also holds *Bull's* P&L — so **every dollar Bull makes silently raises Rocket's core target.** That is lesson 23's disease ("Rocket cannot measure Rocket") reappearing in the *rebalance* rather than the return. **Every rebalance must NAME the base it used.** An unnamed base is the same failure as an undated consensus (11a) or an unsized raise (5a): the label does the reasoning. 🚨 **44a. VIOLATED THE SAME DAY IT WAS WRITTEN** (found 2026-09-02): 9/01's `market_close` struck target_core off the **slice** ($3,179.36), never named the base, and concluded "in band" — **two consecutive sessions on the invalid basis** (8/31 used it and *bought*). Writing a rule into `lessons_learned.md` does not install it in the routine that must obey it. **44b. 🥇 THE ERROR HAS A DIRECTION — it is not noise.** The slice runs **$107.06 richer than the book**, and that gap **is Bull's P&L** (JPM +13.2%, NOW +18.9%) flowing into Rocket's core target through the shared account. **Every dollar Bull makes mechanically pushes Rocket to hold MORE IWM** — the exact position carrying Rocket's entire cumulative deficit to SPY (−2.50% factor drift, six weeks). **The bookkeeping error and the performance problem are the same problem**, which is why this outranks a rounding nit. Escalated to the user, not self-approved.

45. **🆕 🥇 MEASURE THE CATALYST-DAY MULTIPLE ON THE NAME'S OWN HISTORY — NOT A BORROWED ONE** (2026-09-02). Lesson 40a said to apply "a catalyst-day multiple" to trailing range, and two sessions running that meant *guessing* with PD's 2.3× and LTRX's 3.7×, producing a vague band (YEXT: "7.8–12.6%") that decided nothing. **The real number is free and exact: pull 2 years of daily bars, take the widest-range days — those ARE the earnings days — and divide by the median range.** GIII: **4.7×**, last 9 catalyst days **9.8%–19.0%**, *every one* through a 7% trail. DAKT: **4.4%**, ranges 11.3%–19.4%, one gap **−22.9%**. **Both higher than either borrowed multiple**, so the guess was not merely imprecise — it was biased low, i.e. biased toward entering. **45a. Read the earnings-day GAPS, not just the ranges.** A −11.4% gap (GIII) or −22.9% (DAKT) makes stop placement irrelevant: lesson 29's "fills at the open, wherever the open is." **45b. This gate is strictest on QUIET names** (40b) — GIII's 2.3% trailing range is *why* its 4.7× multiple is lethal, and a trailing-range screen alone would have called it the safest name on the board. **45c. It converts a kill into a DATE, not a pass.** A 4.7× range is a *catalyst-day* fact; by day 2 the name reverts toward normal range and fits the trail — so 45 and rule 2 ("enter on the second day," same-day 0-for-3 vs second-day 2-for-3) point the same way. **The output is "not today," not "not ever."**
✅ **45d. VALIDATED AS A PREDICTOR, 2-for-2** (2026-09-03). The 9/02 forecasts were struck
*before* the prints and the tape settled both within ~1.5 points: **GIII predicted ≈14.3%
→ actual 14.6%** (closed −11.5% at **5% of range**); **DAKT predicted ≈13% → actual
14.8%** (gapped +11% to $21.50, closed $19.13 at **5% of range**). **Both exceeded a 7%
trail by ~2×.** The borrowed multiples this rule replaced (PD 2.3×, LTRX 3.7×) would have
predicted 7–11% and **waved both names through.** This gate is no longer just a filter —
it forecasts the range it is filtering on. **45e. But do not over-extend it: rule 45c's
"kill converts to a date" applies ONLY to a range kill.** GIII was ultimately killed by a
*price-action* gate (a below-midpoint close), and price action does not improve overnight.

46. **🆕 🥇 VERIFY ADV ON THE MEDIAN, NOT THE MEAN — A CATALYST-DAY VOLUME SPIKE MANUFACTURES GATE COMPLIANCE ON EXACTLY THE DAY YOU WANT TO TRADE** (2026-09-03). **TLYS** was the first genuine screener signal in six sessions (+29.7%, **23.9× RelVol**, on both lists). `eligibility` read ADV **259,352 — a FAIL** against the 300k gate. Lesson 14 says re-verify near the gate, so I did, and the raw 63-day bars said:

    | Measure | Value | vs 300k |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Mean, as-is** | **326,038** | ✅ **PASSES** |
    | Mean, ex- the 9/02 catalyst bar | 262,491 | ❌ fails |
    | **Median** | **133,200** | ❌ **fails by 56%** |

    **One 4.27M-share bar lifted the 63-day mean through the gate by itself.** A mean-based
    re-verification would have certified a stock whose typical session trades **133k shares
    — under half the minimum** — as liquid, and would have done so *because* of the very
    catalyst being traded. **46a. This is lesson 14 running in reverse, and it is the more
    dangerous direction.** 14's known failure was a mean that was too *low* (RCEL); this is a
    mean too *high*, and it errs toward **entering** an untradeable name rather than skipping
    a tradeable one. **46b. The mechanism is general: any average taken over a window
    containing the catalyst is contaminated by the catalyst.** Use the median for ADV, always.
    **46c. Low float is not a substitute for liquidity — it is the opposite.** TLYS's 11.9M
    float is precisely *why* it moved 29.7% and precisely why a 7% stop cannot be filled.
    It independently failed rule 37a too: **median daily range 7.06% ≈ the entire trail.**
    ✅ **46d. GRADED CORRECT IN ONE SESSION AND FIRED AGAIN THE NEXT** (2026-09-04). TLYS
    printed **−2.3% on 0.8× RelVol** the day after its 23.9× spike — the liquidity kill was

    PASS by 0.9%**; raw bars gave **mean 303,738 (passes), mean ex-catalyst-bar 237,360

    ±10% of the gate, ALSO pull the live bid/ask before doing any further work** — a spread

    **46f. This one cost a genuine rung-1 catalyst** (BBCP: revenue +13%, EBITDA +13%, FY

    overridden, and it was not load-bearing because rule 45 killed the name independently.**
    🆕 🥇 **46g. THE ±10% RE-VERIFY BAND IS A FLOOR, NOT A CEILING — AND THE SPREAD CHECK IS
    CORROBORATION, NOT A KILL ON ITS OWN** (2026-09-09). Two halves, both learned the same
    session, both about a check firing in the wrong regime:
    - **Pull the median even when the mean passes by a WIDE margin.** **OCC** read ADV
      **380,424 — a PASS by 26.8%**, nowhere near lesson 14's ±10% trigger, so nothing would
      have prompted a re-verify. Its **median is 236,900 — a 21% FAIL**, and its last five

      was written to catch *marginal* reads; a contaminated mean can clear the gate by any
      margin at all. **Run the median on every survivor, unconditionally — it is free, the
      bars are already in hand for rule 45.**
    - 🚨 **A wide premarket book is only evidence when the median ADV independently fails.**
      **AVO is the control case**: at 06:25 ET its book read **bid $9.13 / ask $16.12, a 55%
      spread — identical to OCC's — on a median ADV of 865,900 that passes the gate by
      189%.** INNV read 48% wide. At that hour essentially every small cap looks broken, so
      46e **cannot stand alone**: read as a kill it would eliminate every name every
      premarket, which is lesson 38's blank-result trap wearing the opposite sign. **A check
      that always fires carries no information.** OCC's and BBCP's spreads were decisive
      *because* their medians had already failed; AVO's identical spread is discarded.
