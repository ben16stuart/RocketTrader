## 3. Trade review

### FF — −$12.24 (−2.57%), stopped out in 2.5 hours. **The worst decision of the week.**

1. **Was the catalyst real?** Real, and *known to be second-tier at the time*. Revenue
   +120.6%, GAAP net income $11.4M, zero debt — all verified. But premarket ranked it
   **P2 explicitly because there was no raise** (only a qualitative "anticipates positive
   adj. EBITDA") and **no analyst coverage at all**.
2. **Why was it taken?** Because the **P1 (APEI) failed its gate at the open and the slot
   was open.** That is the actual reason, and it is not a reason. FF was entered as a
   *substitute*, not on its own merit. Rocket's own catalyst hierarchy ranks beat-without-
   raise as a known fader (SVCO, CVRX) and Rocket entered one anyway.
3. **The ladder check — Rocket's best filter — could not be run.** No analyst coverage
   means no ladder. It was scored as an absence, effectively a pass. Lesson 6 already says
   *"a 403 or paywall is not a clean bill of health"*; the same logic applies here and was
   not applied. **An un-runnable filter is a FAIL, not a pass.**
4. **Was the exit right?** Yes, unambiguously, and the tape proved it hard: FF closed 8/11
   at **$6.37 (30% of range)** and then went **$5.85 → $5.68 → $5.59**. The stop got Rocket
   out at $6.44; the name is **−13.2% below the exit** three sessions later. **The 7% trail
   did its job perfectly.**

### VELO — −$5.40 (−1.18%), closed same day. **Correct exit; the entry was the error.**

1. **Was the catalyst real?** Yes — genuine beat-and-raise, uncapped ladder, 33% short
   float, dilution structure graded and cleared under lesson 6a. The research was good.
2. **The entry was into a 22.7% opening whipsaw.** VELO opened $17.05, spiked to $17.61,
   round-tripped to **$14.35 within ten minutes**, and Rocket bought the "9:45 base" at
   $15.27 — which was not a base. **It was the middle of a range that had just travelled
   22.7% in fifteen minutes.** That is the single most transferable finding of the week.
3. **Was the exit right? Yes — and the arithmetic is better than it looked.** VELO has
   since recovered to **$16.16 (+7.1% above the exit)**, which reads at first glance like a
   panic sale. It was not. The trailing stop sat at **$14.72** (7% off the $15.83 HWM) and
   **8/13's low was $14.65** — *the stop would have filled at ≈$14.72, a −$16.50 loss.*
   **The discretionary close beat Rocket's own stop by $11.10.** Lesson 4a survives, and
   is now stronger than when it was written.
4. **What the chart said that Rocket ignored:** that a 7% trailing stop is *inside the
   noise band* of a stock with a 22.7% opening range. On that name the stop was not risk
   management, it was a coin flip. **The correct decision was not to enter at all.**

## 6. Rule changes shipped this week

1. **A beat without a raise is a DISQUALIFIER, not a demotion.** 3-for-3 as a fader.
2. **P2 is not a substitute for a failed P1.** If the P1 fails its gate, the default is
   **no satellite**, not the next name down. FF exists only because a slot was open.
3. **An un-runnable filter is a FAIL.** No analyst coverage = no ladder check = skip.
4. **Opening-range gate (NEW):** if the 9:30–9:45 range exceeds **10% of price**, there is
   no tradeable 9:45 base — **defer to the second day.** Validated both ways: VELO's 22.7%
   range would have been deferred to 8/13's $14.95 open, now $16.16 (**+8.1%**); FF's 5.6%
   range would have passed the gate, so this rule is not a cure-all — rules 1–3 are what
   catch FF.
5. **The 9:45–9:50 bar must close in the upper half of its own range.** OMER's did; that
   is what a base looks like.
